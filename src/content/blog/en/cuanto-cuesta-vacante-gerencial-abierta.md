When an analyst role sits open, the team feels it. When a management role sits open, something else stalls: decisions, the pace of the function and, often, the morale of the people who reported to that seat. A headhunter's fee shows up in an email. The cost of not having a leader does not.

In [how to measure the ROI of a hire](/en/blog/roi-contratacion-costo-vacante-abierta) we laid out a framework for tech vacancies. The logic is the same for a manager, a head of or a director: the expensive part is not hiring, it is leaving the seat empty.

## What Finance sees (and what it does not)

What is visible is the cost of hiring: internal time, job posts and, if there is an agency, the fee. That number is real, and in leadership searches it is usually higher than for an individual contributor role. A useful reference, though it comes from the U.S. market, is [SHRM's 2025 recruiting benchmark](https://www.shrm.org/about/press-room/shrm-releases-2025-benchmarking-reports--how-does-your-organizat): the average cost of hiring an executive was $35,879, nearly seven times the cost of a nonexecutive hire ($5,475). It is not a figure to copy into Argentine pesos; it is a signal that searching for a leader costs more than searching for a specialist.

What almost never enters that ledger is the **cost of vacancy**:

- **Decisions that do not get made**, or that get made higher up, with less context, and then have to be corrected.
- **An interim team** covering the role: overtime, lost focus and the risk that someone else leaves.
- **Projects and scoreboards on pause:** a launch, a budget, a reorganization, or a negotiation that depended on that person.
- **The curve once someone finally joins:** onboarding a leader takes longer, because they move people.

None of these items arrives as an invoice. That is why a search "left for later" looks like a saving, and almost never is.

## How to estimate the cost (with your numbers, not someone else's)

The formula is still simple:

**Cost of vacancy = (estimated daily value of the role) × (days the vacancy stays open)**

In a management role, the "daily value" is not the salary you are not paying — that, if anything, is a temporary saving. It is what that role was unblocking: the team's results, the decisions only that seat can sign off, the cost of delay on a project. Fill in the account with your own numbers. In most cases, filling the role faster is cheaper than filling it more slowly.

## How to shorten a leadership search in Argentina

The managers and directors worth hiring are rarely unemployed. They are at another company, often in Buenos Aires or Greater Buenos Aires, and they move if the mandate is clear and the process is serious.

1. **Define the problem of the first 90 days**, not just the title. "Operations manager" does not say whether they are coming in to organize a team, cut costs or scale.
2. **Go after passive talent.** A job post helps as support; it is not enough as a strategy. A [headhunter](/en/blog/que-es-headhunter-cuando-tercerizar-busqueda-tech) makes sense when the role is critical, you need discretion, or the internal team does not have time to map the market.
3. **Compress the process, without skipping references.** Fewer interviews, deeper ones, with the person who actually decides. Checking how they led through a crisis prevents a bad fit, which at this level costs far more than an extra week of evaluation.
4. **Align the package and the band from the start.** Improvising the offer at the end is a common way to lose the candidate. Compensation matters, but so do the team, the reporting line and the real autonomy of the role.
5. **One decision-maker, with a date.** A management search with a committee that does not close looks a lot like having no search at all.

Speed, as with any [role that drags on](/en/blog/cuanto-tarda-cubrir-vacante-tech), is part of the offer: a leader who already has a job will not wait three months for the company to get aligned.
