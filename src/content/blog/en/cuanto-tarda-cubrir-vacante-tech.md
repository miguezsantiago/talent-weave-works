"How long will it take you to fill this position?" is the first question from every leader who opens a search. The honest answer is: it depends. But there are clear patterns and, above all, concrete levers to shorten the timeline.

## The reference number

For mid-to-senior tech roles, time to hire — from the moment the search opens until the candidate accepts — usually falls between **4 and 8 weeks** when the process is well managed. For very scarce roles (highly specific profiles, technical leadership) it can stretch longer.

What matters is not the average, but understanding which stages eat up the time.

## Where the time goes

| Stage | Typical time | Main bottleneck |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Role definition | 2–5 days | Lack of clarity on the "must haves" |
| Sourcing | 1–3 weeks | Relying only on active candidates |
| Interviews | 1–3 weeks | Calendars that don't line up |
| Offer and close | 3–7 days | Misaligned salary bands |

Most delays are not caused by a lack of candidates: they're caused by internal friction.

## Five levers to speed things up

1. **Lock the profile before you start.** Every change of criteria mid-search resets the clock.
2. **Add passive candidates.** The best profiles aren't applying to job posts. Reaching them takes proactive sourcing.
3. **Compress the interview schedule.** Block fixed slots each week so you don't depend on coordinating case by case.
4. **Align the salary band from day one.** A competitive offer avoids weeks of negotiation that end in a "no".
5. **Appoint a single decision-maker.** Processes where three people have to agree slow themselves down.

## The cost of going slow

Every week a key position stays open has a cost: projects that don't move forward, overloaded teams and, often, candidates who leave with another offer. Speeding up the process is not just about efficiency: it's about protecting the business.
