"Why pay a headhunter if I can post the job myself?" It's the right question, and the answer isn't always "you should outsource". But there are specific moments when an in-house search stalls and a tech headhunter stops being an expense and becomes the fastest way to solve the problem.

## What a headhunter does (and doesn't do)

A headhunter is not someone who posts your job on more boards. Their core work is **going after candidates who aren't looking for a job**: passive profiles, already employed, who won't show up on Bumeran, ZonaJobs or Computrabajo no matter how well the post is written. That includes:

- Mapping the market and building a list of real targets, instead of waiting for applications.
- Contacting, qualifying and filtering before showing you a single shortlist.
- Handling the salary negotiation and the close, including counteroffers.
- Knowing up-to-date salary bands — with references like the [SysArmy salary survey](https://sueldos.openqube.io) — so you don't lose the candidate over a misaligned offer.

What they don't do: replace your final judgment. You always choose who joins the team.

## In-house vs. job board vs. headhunter

| | In-house search / job board | Tech headhunter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Candidate pool | Active only (they apply to the post) | Active + passive (most of the market) |
| Speed on scarce roles | Weeks of waiting with no results | Proactive sourcing from day one |
| Cost if the role stays empty | Hidden, but real (stalled projects) | Explicit, but bounded and guaranteed |
| Knowledge of salary bands | Depends on who runs the search | Up to date from working the market daily |

## Five signs it's time to outsource

1. **You've gone more than 3-4 weeks without qualified candidates.** If the post isn't bringing anyone in, the problem isn't the post: the profile you're looking for isn't applying to job posts.
2. **The role is scarce or very specific.** Technical leadership, uncommon stacks and high seniority live in the passive market, not on job boards. Talent shortage surveys by [Randstad](https://www.randstad.com.ar/tendencias-360) and [ManpowerGroup](https://www.manpowergroup.com) have been pointing this out for years: it's getting harder and harder to fill technical roles with traditional search.
3. **Nobody on the team has time for sourcing.** Recruiting well is a full-time job. If your HR team is already stretched thin, every extra week of searching costs more than outsourcing it.
4. **You've had a bad hire recently.** The cost of a poor fit (time, money, team morale) is usually higher than the fee of a headhunter with a replacement guarantee.
5. **You need discretion.** Replacing someone, running a parallel search or expanding into a new market without it becoming public requires a third party.

If none of these apply and the role is high-turnover (junior profiles, in-demand roles with plenty of supply), a well-run in-house search is probably enough.

## What to ask before hiring a headhunter

- Do they charge on success (a fee at close) or upfront?
- What guarantee do they offer if the candidate doesn't last the first few months?
- Do they know the Argentina/LATAM tech market specifically, or are they a generalist headhunter?
- How many shortlists have they put together for roles like yours in recent months?

A good tech headhunter answers these questions directly and shows you concrete cases, not generic promises.
